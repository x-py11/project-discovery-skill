# Design Principles

## 1. Minimize avoidable misunderstanding
Discovery is not about collecting every detail; it is about surfacing unknowns likely to cause a mismatch.

## 2. Questions should earn their place
Every question costs time and attention. Ask when an answer can materially improve the result or prevent expensive rework.

## 3. Use defaults responsibly
Defaults should be coherent, reversible where possible, and disclosed when they materially shape the outcome.

## 4. User control is adjustable
Some users want collaborative decisions; others want end-to-end delegation. Adapt to the user's preferred level of involvement.

## 5. Separate facts from assumptions
Do not turn guesses into requirements. Keep confirmed decisions, assumptions, and open questions distinct.

## 6. Discovery must lead to delivery
Clarification is a means, not the product. Once the direction is clear enough, move forward and revise when new information warrants it.

## 7. Be platform-aware
Instructions cannot override host capabilities, safety rules, or authorization requirements.
