# Question Frameworks

Use these prompts as a menu, not a checklist. Ask only what is relevant and not already answered.

## Universal
- What are you hoping to achieve?
- Who is it for, and what should they understand, feel, or do afterward?
- What would make the result successful?
- Is there a reference or direction you already like?
- Are there hard requirements, limitations, or deadlines?
- Would you prefer to choose together, or should I recommend a direction?

## Presentation
Consider audience, purpose, duration or slide count, narrative style, visual style, required content, source material, citations, and output format.

## Website or software
Consider target users, main workflows, must-have features, platform, integrations, data handling, accessibility, privacy, acceptance criteria, and what counts as a usable first version. Explain technical trade-offs instead of expecting the user to choose unfamiliar technologies.

## Image, poster, logo, or visual design
Consider subject, message, audience, intended use, visual style, composition, aspect ratio, resolution, required text, logos, reference images, and whether editable source files are needed. Separate creative choices from exact constraints.

## Writing, report, proposal, or essay
Consider purpose, audience, tone, length, language, structure, required sources, evidence standards, citations, and submission format.

## Video, animation, or audio
Consider platform, audience, purpose, duration, aspect ratio, tone, pacing, voice-over, captions, music, available assets, and final format.

## Data analysis
Consider the question to answer, data source, definitions, units, time range, comparison groups, output format, data quality, and limitations. Avoid claiming causation from correlation.

## Learning material or mind map
Consider learner level, learning objectives, exact scope, format, detail level, examples, exercises, language, page size, and export format.

## Business or product planning
Consider target users, problem, value proposition, resources, timeline, budget, constraints, success measures, and whether the user wants exploration or a decision-ready recommendation. Do not present speculation as validated evidence.

## 3D, game, or interactive experience
Consider platform, audience, core interaction, art direction, references, scope, mechanics, assets, technical constraints, and prototype versus production expectations.

## When not to ask more
Do not ask just because a detail is missing. Use a reasonable, reversible default when the detail is low-impact, the user delegated decisions, the request is already clear, or a small draft can reveal preferences faster than another question. Ask about high-impact, hard-to-reverse choices.
